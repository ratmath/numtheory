Sums of squares

Which numbers are sums of two squares, of three, of four? Fermat, Legendre and Lagrange answered each, and Jacobi counted the ways. Every representation of n as a sum of two squares comes out of the factorisation of n in the Gaussian integers.

Two squares

Fermat: an odd prime is a sum of two squares exactly when p≡1(mod4)p \equiv 1 \pmod 4p≡1(mod4). To find them, take xxx with x2≡−1(modp)x^2 \equiv -1 \pmod px2≡−1(modp) and run Euclid's algorithm on ppp and xxx; the first remainder below p\sqrt pp​ is aaa, and p−a2p - a^2p−a2 is a square (Cornacchia, Brillhart).
A number nnn is a sum of two squares exactly when every prime ≡3(mod4)\equiv 3 \pmod 4≡3(mod4) divides it to an even power.

Three squares and four

Legendre and Gauss: nnn is a sum of three squares unless n=4a(8b+7)n = 4^a(8b + 7)n=4a(8b+7). Lagrange: every nnn is a sum of four. Jacobi again counts:

Pythagorean triples
